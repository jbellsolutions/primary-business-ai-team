# Quiet-day list

Serves asks #16 and #17. Follow SOP 06, the downtime checklist.

## Inputs
- Todoist: open, waiting and missed tasks, with their comments. Read through the app, or Adrielle pastes them.
- Chris's calendar ahead, up to and including the next trip and the next workshops.
- The admin inbox since the last triage, and the For Adrielle flag.
- `owner.md`: the project names, travel preferences, and who the co-hosts and subcontractors are by first name.
- The SOP index, and the Unknowns list if Adrielle keeps one (SOP 20).

## Steps
1. Test that it is a quiet day. Adrielle's list is clear and Chris is busy or away, or she has asked. If work is waiting, say what it is and stop. This list is for gaps and not for busy days.
2. Check these in order. For each, report what you found, or "checked, nothing":
   - **Old tasks.** Re-read open and waiting tasks and their comments for a detail missed. This is Chris's "go back through your tasks".
   - **Flagged mail.** Anything new with the For Adrielle flag (inbox-triage).
   - **Look-ahead.** For each coming trip or workshop: travel, stay, venue, flyer and website event. Is each done and confirmed? A gap becomes a task line.
   - **Agendas.** For each coming meeting with no agenda, a one-line draft agenda for Chris to confirm.
   - **Travel.** Is it booked, confirmed and on the calendar (calendar-entry)?
   - **Event prep.** What can start early for the next workshop. From M2 Workshops & Travel drafts it. Until then follow SOP 11.
   - **Email.** Read and summarise what has built up (inbox-triage).
   - **Xero unknowns.** Lines Adrielle can code, and the Unknowns list to update (SOP 20).
   - **Filing and website.** Loose files (SOP 28) and the website tidy-up (SOP 26).
   - **SOP gaps.** One task done lately with no SOP, so Chris can be asked to record it (SOP 29; see sop-gap-check).
   - **One improvement idea** for Chris's suggestions list (check with Chris), kept on handover-list or automation-backlog.
3. Keep an item only if it is real. Each item names who needs the result, or the date it serves, and what it produces. Drop anything that is only a way to look busy.
4. Check before you list it. If Chris has already handled it, say "already done" and move on.
5. Order by date, with the nearest trip or workshop first. Anything that needs a decision goes into the end-of-day "Needs your yes".
6. If nothing real is left, say so: "Nothing needs doing. Stay reachable by phone and Teams." That is a good answer (ask #17).
7. Check (#66): every item has a source, a result and a doer.

## Output
A list of up to seven items:
- the item;
- why it is real (the date or the person it serves);
- what it produces;
- who does it.

Under it, one line headed "What I checked", so Adrielle can show Chris what was checked and prepared (SOP 06 done). If there is nothing, that line and the sentence in step 6.

## Done when
Every item is a real job with a result and a doer, or the list says nothing needs doing.

## Never
- Make work, or suggest sitting at the computer in case Chris calls (ask #17).
- Monitor Adrielle's screen or activity. Chris judges output (ask #83).
- Book, send, post or pay as a way to fill time. Those need Chris's yes.
- Chase Chris for new work.
- Count "checked, nothing" as a failure.
