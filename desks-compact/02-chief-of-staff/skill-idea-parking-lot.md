# Idea parking lot

Serves #18: the assistant flags an idea and asks whether to look into it; Chris adds the background; if his "brain is just full", it is written down and he is reminded next week.

## Inputs
- The idea, in Chris's words (typed or dictated), or passed on by Adrielle.
- `AI Team/notebook/Parking-lot.md`, the parking-lot table that Adrielle saves. A copy sits in this Project's files if she has loaded one. If you can read neither, write "Parking lot: UNKNOWN, not readable" and work from what Chris pastes.
- The calendar, to pick a reminder date that is not a workshop or travel day.

## Steps
1. **Say it back in one line**, using his words. Ask nothing else yet.
2. **Ask one question:** "Look into this now, or park it for next week?" Offer both as choices. Do nothing until he answers.
3. **If he says look into it:** ask for the background he has, one question at a time: what prompted it, what he wants out of it, who or which project it touches, and whether there is a date. Don't spend effort until he has given the context. Then write a scoped brief of what to check and which desk can do it (Research & Data for a literature check, Projects & Growth for a funding or contract angle, Farmer Content for a post). The brief is a draft for Adrielle to pass on, not work started.
4. **If he says park it, or says his brain is full:** accept it without comment. Write one row for Adrielle to save, and give it now, in this chat. Set "Remind on" to the Monday of next week, or the date he names. If that day has a workshop or a long drive, move it to the next day with neither and say so.
5. **If the idea is something he wants the team to do**, not only think about, say so in one line. Adrielle adds it to the ask ledger (skill ask-ledger).
6. **When a reminder falls due:** Adrielle raises it at the daily catch-up from the file she keeps. You add your own reminder only if you can read the parking lot, and if you can't, you say so rather than imply one is set. Put it in the Morning brief as one line (never more than one idea in a brief), and under "Parked ideas due" in the Friday weekly review. Ask one question: "Look into it now, push it a week, or drop it?" Pushing is fine. After a third push, ask once whether to drop it. Dropped means status "dropped" in the table; it stays on the record.
7. **Check pass.** The row quotes his words, the date is a real day, and nothing in it is your own idea presented as his.

## Output
One row for Adrielle to add to `AI Team/notebook/Parking-lot.md`, under the header HANDOFFS · FOR ADRIELLE (paste to Adrielle when convenient). If the file doesn't exist yet, she creates it with this header first:
```
| Date parked | Idea | Chris's words | Background | Remind on | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| <YYYY-MM-DD> | <idea, one line> | "<his exact words>" | <background, or "not given"> | <YYYY-MM-DD> | parked |
```
Status is `parked`, `looking into`, `done` or `dropped`. Then one line to Chris: "Parked. The row is above for Adrielle to save, and she'll raise it <day>." Add "I'll bring it back too" only if you can read the parking lot.

## Done when
The idea is in a row Adrielle can save, with a reminder date that is not a workshop or travel day, and Chris has not been asked for more than one thing at a time.

## Never
- Start research on an idea Chris has not said to look into.
- Fill in the background yourself. Unknown stays "not given".
- Remind him more than once for the same date, or push an idea at him on a workshop day.
- Delete a row. Dropped ideas stay.
- Present your own idea as Chris's.
