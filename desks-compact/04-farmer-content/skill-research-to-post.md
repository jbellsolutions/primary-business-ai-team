# Research to post

Serves #40 ("there's a new bit of research out. Let's push this out") and #43 (articles on the website: Chris writes, Adrielle posts). Where new research is published (#47) and whether a newsletter is wanted (#46) are open, so this skill makes no newsletter.

## Inputs
- The finding as Chris gives it: a report section, a summary he approved, a slide, or his words. Not raw data.
- `owner.md`: the project, its data rule (#90), whether anyone else must see or approve a result before it goes public, and the funder acknowledgement line if there is one.
- Any chart for it, built by honest-chart and signed by Chris.
- `post-log.md`.

## Steps
1. **Is it cleared?** Chris must have said, about this finding, that it can go public. Then check `owner.md` for the project: if it says another OK is needed first, or says nothing, ask Chris: "Does <project> need anyone else's OK before this goes public?" Until it is cleared the finding stays private (the data rules).
2. **Take the finding apart** into five lines:
   - what was tested;
   - what was found, the number or result exactly as Chris states it, with units;
   - what it means on the farm, only if Chris said so;
   - what it doesn't show: his limits on conditions, season and sample;
   - where to read more.
   Any line the source doesn't give becomes `MISSING: ask Chris`. Don't fill it.
3. **Rewrite in plain English** using the words Chris uses on the websites and in his slides. Anything taken from the project website is the project's material, so farmer-post step 8 (the funder line) applies. Keep every number and unit identical, with no rounding. Keep the strength of the claim: "showed" stays "showed", never "proved".
4. **Draft up to two posts** with farmer-post: one "what's new" post, and one "what it means on the farm" post for a different day. Platform-neutral until `owner.md` names the platforms.
5. **Draft the article outline** for website-post. Outline only. Chris writes the article.
   - Working title and the one-line takeaway.
   - Headings: what we looked at, what we found, what it means on the farm, what to do next, more information.
   - Where each chart, table or photo goes, and the links to include.
6. **Make the claims table.** One row per number, unit or comparison in the posts and outline: Claim · Source line · Match? Anything that doesn't match is fixed or marked `ASK`.
7. **Hand over:** the posts go to Chris for "Approve or change?"; the outline goes with them. Any chart needs Chris's signed checklist before it appears anywhere.
8. **Newsletter:** if Chris asks for one, say it's open (#46) and that farmers don't want email (#41). Offer to reuse the outline for whatever channel he picks.

## Output
The finding card (five lines), up to two post drafts, the article outline, the claims table, and a list of `ASK` items for Chris.

## Done when
Every claim in the posts and outline matches a source line, every gap is marked, and Chris has the pack waiting for his yes.

## Never
- Use a result Chris hasn't cleared, or unpublished data.
- Infer a result, merge two studies, or turn a trend into a conclusion.
- Add a recommendation Chris didn't make.
- Write the article for Chris to sign as his own, or publish anything.
- Name a funder from memory.
