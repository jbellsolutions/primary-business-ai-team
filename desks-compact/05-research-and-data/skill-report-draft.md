# Report draft

Serves #53 (a trial: draft a report from his notes) and #48 (Justin's team recommends that a person reviews every draft before it gets pushed, and that is the rule here). Chris's research is physical work in the field, so this skill never does research. It drafts report text from the notes and evidence he gives it, and only when he asks. Follows SOP 24 (Project report collation). The findings and recommendations are Chris's.

## Inputs
- Chris's notes: typed, dictated, or a call recap Adrielle pastes.
- The tables, charts and summaries he points to: honest-chart output with a signed checklist, survey-summary pages, the figures from his workbook.
- The template and required headings (`check with Chris` where they live, SOP 24 step 1).
- `owner.md`: the project, the deliverable, and its data rule (the lines "Research data in AI tools" and "Participant details in AI tools", ask #90).

## Steps
1. **Scope in one question.** Which report, which sections, who reads it. Ask only what the files don't answer.
2. **Data rule first.** A yes: carry on. A no: stop, and offer a headings skeleton with `[CHRIS TO WRITE]` in every box. Nothing recorded: work only with notes that have participant names, contact details and farm or site identifiers removed, ask "Is AI allowed on <project>'s report material?" in the same message, and label the draft provisional.
3. **Build the skeleton from the required headings.** Beside each heading, say whether the notes cover it: yes, partly, no.
4. **Write only what the notes and attached evidence support.**
   - Tag every paragraph with its source in brackets: [notes, <date>, para 3], [Table 2], [Survey Q4 summary].
   - If a heading has no support, write `[CHRIS TO WRITE: <what is needed>]`. Never fill it.
5. **Findings and recommendations.** List the findings the notes state, in Chris's own statements. Leave a `[CHRIS: recommendations]` box. Don't infer a recommendation from data. In SOP 24, "he writes the recommendations".
6. **Numbers.** Copy them exactly, with units, and tie each to its source tag. Build tables from the provided cells by code or Excel. Charts come only from honest-chart.
7. **Mark it everywhere.** The first line of every page of the document says "DRAFT for Chris's review. Not checked for accuracy. Not for sharing." The file name ends -DRAFT.
8. **Write the review pack, and put it first:**
   - every number and its source, and every claim and its tag;
   - the assumptions you made, and the gaps;
   - any place where two notes disagree. Show both versions and leave the choice to Chris.
9. **After Chris edits,** his version is the master. report-format then formats it. Keep versions in the file name.

## Output
The marked draft with source tags, and the review pack: numbers and sources, assumptions, gaps, contradictions.

## Done when
Every paragraph has a source tag or a `CHRIS TO WRITE` box, the review pack is complete, and the draft is with Chris. Nothing else has happened to it.

## Never
- Do the research, or present a draft as Chris's findings. The findings come from his physical work and his notes.
- Invent data, a quote, a citation, a date or a result.
- Write or infer a recommendation or conclusion.
- Smooth over a contradiction, or sound more certain than the notes.
- Remove the DRAFT marking, or send, submit or share the report.
- Include participant names or contact details.
