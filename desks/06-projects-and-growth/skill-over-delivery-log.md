# Over-delivery log

Serves #54, and feeds the next contract Chris goes after (#56, #55). One log per project, saved by Adrielle as `AI Team/projects/<project>/over-delivery.md`. Nothing counts as proof until Chris has confirmed it.

## Inputs
- `owner.md`: the project's deliverables and report dates, as Chris confirmed them. This is the "promised" baseline.
- The existing `over-delivery.md` for the project (Adrielle pastes it or it is read from OneDrive).
- What happened: Daily notes in `AI Team/notebook/Daily/`, the calendar entries in the project's category, workshop attendance and evaluation summaries Adrielle pastes from Airtable, and anything Chris says at the catch-up.
- Funder feedback in the admin inbox or Teams, only when the words exist in writing.
- Ask #90's answer, the line `Project documents for ChatGPT (#90): yes` in `owner.md`, if the evidence sits inside project documents. Without it, use only what Chris or Adrielle tells you.

## Steps
1. Find the baseline. No promised deliverables for the project in `owner.md` means "NO_CLIENT_SOURCE: deliverables for <project>"; ask Adrielle for them and stop for that project.
2. Take each candidate item (something done this period) and test it against the baseline. Promised: it is in the deliverable list, or the funder asked for it in writing, including a later variation. Beyond: it was done and not required. Unclear: ask Chris one question.
3. Keep only what Chris would be glad to say aloud to the funder. Routine work, and effort for its own sake, is not over-delivery. Ordinary admin never goes in.
4. Write each entry in this form:
   - Date and a five-word title.
   - Promised: the deliverable it relates to, or "not in the contract".
   - Delivered: what was done, in plain words.
   - Beyond: how it goes past the promise.
   - Evidence: the file, email subject, meeting, evaluation or Airtable row, by name.
   - Status: Proposed, or Confirmed by Chris on <date>.
5. Numbers (attendance, counts, dates) go in only as a source gives them, with the source named. Never estimate or round up.
6. Funder feedback: record the exact words, the date, the channel and the speaker's role. If you only remember the gist, leave it out.
7. Mark evidence strength: Documented (a file or message exists), Reported by Chris (told at the catch-up, no document), or Needs a source.
8. Add a short headline per project: the three strongest Confirmed entries, and the count of Confirmed beyond-contract entries against the promised deliverables done. Count from the log, and say it is a count of entries, not a measure of value.
9. List what Chris must confirm: one line per Proposed entry.

## Output
- New or changed entries, ready for Adrielle to paste into `over-delivery.md`, in order of date.
- The "Chris to confirm" list, one line per entry.
- The per-project headline, and any project with no entries (stated as no entries yet, not as a problem).

## Done when
Every entry has a baseline, an evidence pointer and a status; no number is estimated; and Chris's confirmation is recorded against each entry marked Confirmed.

## Never
- Invent a result, number, quote, testimonial or funder reaction.
- Count promised work, or work the funder asked for, as extra.
- Mark an entry Confirmed without Chris's own words.
- Mix one project's log or evidence into another's without Chris's yes.
- Share the log outside the business.
